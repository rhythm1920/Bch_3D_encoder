3-D BCH encoder · N = 15, T = 2 · GF(16)

Three encoder architectures, after place and route

Each design maps 3,163 message bits to a 3,375-bit codeword (15×15×15). Tool flow: Vivado 2026.1, out of context, flatten rebuilt.

Design 1 · baseline

Follows Mondal & Garani 2021. Every output point has its own conjugate-class multipliers and an XOR array, so nothing is shared. One codeword per clock.

Full design not run; 225-point slice routed
Design 2 · shared multipliers

Compute once, route many. One bank of computation-class products (11,500 wires) feeds 3,375 XOR trees. One codeword per clock.

182 k LUTs, failed routing (congestion)
Design 3 · time-multiplexed

Spectral registers are rotated by α−j3 each cycle. 225 XOR trees are reused over 15 cycles, and an assembly register collects the planes. One codeword per 15 clocks.

17 k LUTs, routed on Virtex-7 and Artix-7

Design 2 is not limited by its LUT count, since it uses only 15% of the device. Every one of its trees needs wires from across the whole message, the router's congestion peaked at 99.8%, and 66,912 routing nodes remained overlapping. Design 1 has the same wiring pattern with three times the logic, so its full run was skipped. Its numbers are the measured slice scaled ×15. Design 3 is the only full encoder that fits and routes, at roughly 1/10 of design 2's area.
